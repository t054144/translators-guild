How to use this handbook

كيف تستخدم هذا الدليل

This handbook is a working companion for translating between English and Arabic, in both directions. It was prepared for Guild Season workshops, where our Translation Team uses it with students on real texts.

The three dots

The three dots of the Guild mark stand for the three phases every translation in this book goes through. Each phase has its own part of the handbook and its own checklist.

1 · Analyse
Read the text, its author, audience and purpose before writing a word. Ch. 14–15
2 · Translate
Choose a type, procedure or strategy for each problem you meet. Ch. 6–10
3 · Comment
Justify your choices, revise, and check the risks. Ch. 16–17

How each chapter is built

  • A Key terms box opens every chapter, with each term defined and given its Arabic equivalent. Every one of these terms also appears in the Glossary (p. 38).
  • Examples appear in pairs. The source text is on the left and the translation on the right, with the direction marked.
  • Chapters are short on purpose. Read one, then practise (pp. 35–37).

Before you translate

نصائح وتقنيات وإعداد للترجمة

Most translation mistakes begin before the first sentence is written, when a translator starts without knowing who the text is for. Answer three questions first. Together they make up your translation brief (Nord 1997).

Who is the client?من هو العميل؟Who commissioned the work, and what are their house style, policy and ideology? A news agency, a court and a ministry each expect different things.
What is the purpose?ما الغرض التواصلي؟Will the text inform, persuade, instruct, bind legally or entertain? The purpose decides how far you may move away from the form.
Who is the audience?من هو الجمهور المستهدف؟Experts or the public? Gulf readers or pan-Arab readers? Adults or children? Pitch your terms, register and explanations to match.

Preparation

  1. Read the whole text twice before you translate. Each reading brings out new ideas.
  2. Mark the hard parts: unfamiliar terms, idioms, names, figures and cultural references.
  3. Research the field. Read a parallel text written in the TL on the same topic.
  4. Build a mini-glossary before drafting (Ch. 11), and prepare your dictionaries (Ch. 12).
  5. Plan your time, with at least one pause between drafting and revising.

Working habits

  • Fidelity (الأمانة): be faithful to the meaning, not to the words.
  • Aim for three qualities: faithful, accurate and acceptable to TL readers.
  • Translation is rewriting. The TT has to read as though it were written in the TL.
  • Revise every time, even your best work. Read the TT once without the ST.
  • Translate captions, titles and footnotes too. Nothing is “too small” to check.
  • Quality + speed = productivity. Speed that costs quality is not productivity.
Guild tip. Translation is often compared to a minefield (الترجمة حقل ألغام). A single wrong word, such as a politically loaded place name or a false friend in a contract, can harm your client and your reputation. When in doubt, look it up, ask, or flag it. Never guess.
