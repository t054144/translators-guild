Part IV · Practice · Phase 1: Analyse

Text analysis and genres

تحليل النصوص وأجناسها
Key termsمفاهيم أساسية
Text النص
Any stretch of language that communicates: a word, a sentence, a paragraph or a book.
Text analysis تحليل النص
Breaking a text down and processing it before translation, to identify its features, background and problems.
Genre الجنس النصّي
A conventional type of text with recognisable features, such as a news report, a contract or a poem.
Tone النبرة
The writer’s attitude: neutral, critical, persuasive, ironic and so on.

Why analyse first?

  • Every new reading brings out new ideas and problems.
  • It identifies the field, genre, tone, style, time and purpose of the text.
  • It identifies the audience of both the ST and the TT.
  • It shows whether you are ready, or need more resources.
  • It lets you pick your strategies in advance.
  • It gives you a mental draft of the translation, so you work faster.
Analysis التحليل
Identifies the features, background and main ideas of the text: who wrote it, for whom, how and why.
Summary التلخيص
Gives only the facts of what the text is about. Do not summarise or paraphrase when asked to analyse.

Worked example: a KUNA news report

FieldPolitical news, with Kuwait and Australia as the topic.
Author / publisherKUNA (Kuwait News Agency), the state news agency. Follow its terminology and house style.
AudienceReaders interested in Kuwait and the Middle East.
Tone and styleStraightforward and formal. Use formal language and avoid short forms.
Word choiceWatch for connotation, e.g. government rents, and for specialised terms.
StrategyMostly literal and communicative, since the text has no metaphors. Keep the headline short, with no additions.
Guild tip. Your analysis and your translation must agree. If the analysis calls the tone formal, a single colloquial word in your TT will be noticed. Flag every problem in the analysis, then solve it in the translation.

How to write a comprehensive analysis

  1. Identify the text type, its purpose and its genre, e.g. an informative news report.
  2. Place it: author or speaker, publisher, date and place, and the ST and TT audiences.
  3. Describe it: field, tone, style, register and length.
  4. List the problems: terms, idioms, culture-specific items and ideologically loaded words.
  5. Choose strategies for each problem, and note the resources you will need.
Headlines and titles
Keep them as short as possible and never add information. Arabic headlines prefer the present tense.
Kuwait, Australia sign trade deal→الكويت وأستراليا توقّعان اتفاقية تجارية
Hybrid texts
A single text can mix genres, for example a news report that quotes a Qur’anic verse and a legal clause. Treat each part by the rules of its own genre, and use an established translation for the verse.

Be a good critic. Build your own analysis model from Checklist 1 (p. 33) and train it on every text. It is the fastest way to improve translation quality and proficiency.

Part IV · Practice · Critical discourse analysis

Fairclough’s relational model

التحليل النقدي للخطاب: نموذج فيركلف العلائقي
Key termsمفاهيم أساسية
Discourse الخطاب
Language in use, seen as a form of social practice.
CDA التحليل النقدي للخطاب
An approach that studies how language produces, keeps up or resists power and ideology.
Ideology الأيديولوجيا
A set of beliefs and values that shapes how events are represented.
Intertextuality التناصّ
The way a text draws on and echoes other texts.

Fairclough (1989, 1992) analyses every text in three nested dimensions. He later calls this a dialectical-relational approach (Fairclough 2009), because text, discursive practice and society constantly shape one another.

A translation is itself a discursive practice. Your choices can reproduce or neutralise the ideology of the ST.

Ideology in translators’ choices

regime · governmentنظام · حكومةThe first delegitimises
militants · terrorists · fightersمسلّحون · إرهابيون · مقاتلونEach takes a stance
Protesters were killed. (no agent)قُتل متظاهرونThe passive hides who did it

Checklist · Fairclough for translators

1 · Text

  • Which words carry evaluation or connotation?
  • Who is the agent, and who is hidden by the passive?
  • Which metaphors frame the issue?

2 · Discursive practice

  • Who produced the text, for which outlet?
  • Which other texts does it quote or echo?

3 · Social practice

  • Whose interests does the text serve?
  • What ideology or power relation does it reflect?

Your translation

  • Have I kept, softened or intensified the ST’s stance, and why?
  • Does my choice follow the client’s policy?
  • Have I flagged loaded terms in the commentary?

Part IV · Practice · Phase 3: Comment

Translation commentary

التعليق على الترجمة وكتابة الملاحظات
Key termsمفاهيم أساسية
Translation commentary التعليق على الترجمة
A reflective text in which the translator explains the problems met in a translation and justifies the solutions chosen.
Translation criticism نقد الترجمة
Evaluating a translation, your own or someone else’s, against the ST and the brief.

Structure

Part one: reflect on your analysis. Cover the text type, audience, purpose and the main problems you expected.

Part two: reflect on your translation. For each problem, give the problem, then the solution, then the justification, with examples from the ST and TT.

Useful phrases

The ST features…
To preserve the…, the translator opted for…
A functional equivalent was preferred over a formal one because…
This detail was omitted because it is irrelevant to the target audience.
The term was verified in UNTERM and Al-Mawrid.
The loaded term X was rendered as Y, following the client’s style guide.

Rules

  • Write in the third person: “The translator faced…”
  • Always quote examples from the ST and TT.
  • Name the procedure or strategy (Ch. 7–8).
  • Mention the dictionaries and resources you used.
  • Answer the WH questions: what was the problem, why did it arise, how was it solved, and with what effect?

A model commentary paragraph

The ST describes the agreement as having “remained a dead letter”. A formal rendering, حرفاً ميتاً, would have kept the image but puzzled Arab readers. The translator therefore chose the functional equivalent حبراً على ورق, an established Arabic idiom that does the same job and suits the formal register of the news report. The choice was checked against Arabic press usage.

Critiquing someone else’s translation

Compare the ST and TT segment by segment. Note every shift (additions, omissions, changes of meaning or register), then judge the TT against five criteria: accuracy · terminology · fluency · style · fitness for the brief. Separate real errors from legitimate preferences.

Interlingual risk assessment

مصفوفة تقييم المخاطر بين اللغتين
Key termsمفاهيم أساسية
Risk الخطر
The chance that a translation decision causes harm, multiplied by how serious that harm would be (Pym 2015).
Likelihood · Impact
How likely an error is · how much damage it would do to the client, the reader or you.
Mitigation التخفيف
An action that lowers the risk, such as research, a second reviewer, back-translation or a note to the client.

How to use it. During revision, list the risky items in your TT and place each one in the grid.

  • Critical / High Research it again, get a second reviewer, and flag it to the client.
  • Medium Check it against a reliable source.
  • Low Accept it, and note it if relevant.

Practical checklists

قوائم التحقّق العملية
● Checklist 1 · Before you translate · Analysis
  • Client and brief: who asked, and why?
  • Purpose of the TT: to inform, persuade, instruct or bind?
  • Audience of the ST and of the TT
  • Author / speaker and publisher
  • Field and genre
  • Tone and style of writing
  • Terms highlighted and researched
  • Idioms, metaphors and culture-specific items marked
  • Loaded words noted (Ch. 15)
  • Strategies chosen; resources ready
● Checklist 2 · After you translate · Revision and quality
  • Nothing omitted or added without a reason
  • Meaning preserved, including every negation
  • Terms consistent with the glossary
  • Names, numbers, dates and units checked
  • Collocations natural in the TL
  • Arabic: verb-first where natural; hamza, ة/ه and ى/ي correct
  • English: articles, tenses and capitals correct
  • Punctuation suited to the TL (، ؛ ؟)
  • Titles and captions translated
  • TT read once without the ST
● Checklist 3 · Commentary and risk
  • Part one reflects on the analysis
  • Part two covers each problem, its solution and the justification
  • Examples quoted from the ST and TT
  • Procedures and strategies named
  • Written in the third person
  • Resources and dictionaries cited
  • High-risk items flagged (Ch. 17)
  • MT or AI use disclosed, if any

Answer key

These are model answers, not the only correct ones. Any rendering that is accurate, natural and fits the brief is acceptable.

Level 1

1زارت جدّتها.Collocation: the verb زار, not دفعت زيارة.
2ظلّت الاتفاقية حبراً على ورق.A functional equivalent (Farghal).
3يدخّن بشراهة.Transposition: adjective + noun becomes verb + adverbial.
4تمطر بغزارة / كأفواه القِرَب.An idiom never goes word for word.
5زار وزيرُ الداخلية البريطاني الكويتَ أمس.Cultural approximation of a title; verb-first.
6تخضع هذه الاتفاقية لقوانين دولة الكويت.Legal shall becomes the Arabic present tense, not سوف.
7He came back empty-handed.A proverb rendered by equivalence.
8The news warmed my heart.The image is reversed, from cold to warm (Ch. 9).
9He is exceptionally brave.Omission of the synonym pair.
10The diwaniya, a regular gathering held in a home’s reception hall, is central to social life in Kuwait.Borrowing + description.
11I am growing worried about the results.An Arabic collocation made natural in English.
12Press and hold the power button.Technical imperative; مطوّلاً becomes hold.

Level 2a · model

أعلن مسؤولون يوم الأحد أنّ المكتبة المركزية في المدينة ستمدّد ساعات عملها خلال العطلة الصيفية، إذ ستظلّ المكتبة، التي تضمّ أكثر من 200 ألف عنوان، مفتوحةً حتى العاشرة مساءً في أيام العمل. وأوضح المسؤولون أنّ هذه الخطوة تهدف إلى تشجيع الشباب على القراءة، مضيفين أنه ستُعقد أيضاً ورشُ عملٍ مجانية في الكتابة الإبداعية والترجمة.

Points: the reporting clause moves to the front; the verb comes first; adding that becomes مضيفين أنّ; stay open becomes تظلّ مفتوحةً.

Level 3b · model

Last night, the Cultural Society honoured a group of outstanding young translators at an evening attended by writers and academics. In his speech, the society’s president described translation as a bridge between peoples, adding that caring for young translators is a duty, not a luxury. The evening closed with readings of translated poetry in a warm, appreciative atmosphere.

Points: the long sentence is split; لفيف من becomes attended by; the pair الودّ والتقدير becomes one phrase; the metaphor جسر is kept because the image is shared.
