How to use this handbook

كيف تستخدم هذا الدليل

This handbook is for beginners. You do not need to know anything about translation to use it. It explains the basics of translating between English and Arabic, one step at a time.

The order of the book

The chapters follow the order in which a translator actually works:

Part 5 introduces useful tools and text types. Part 6 gives you exercises, with answers.

How each chapter works

Every chapter has the same simple layout, so you always know where to look:

  1. Introduction: one sentence on what you will learn.
  2. A quotation from a translator or scholar, in most chapters.
  3. In plain words: the main idea, explained with an everyday comparison.
  4. Key words: the new terms, with their Arabic equivalents.
  5. Explanation: short sections with examples in both languages.
  6. Remember: the most important points.

New to translation? Read the next page first: it walks you through one complete translation.

Your first translation, step by step

ترجمتك الأولى خطوةً خطوة

Before you read the chapters, watch the whole process once. Here is one short sentence, translated in the five steps this book follows.

It’s raining cats and dogs, so the match has been cancelled.
1

Prepare Chapter 3

Ask the three questions. Client: a school. Purpose: to inform. Audience: Arabic-speaking parents. So the Arabic should be clear, simple and in standard Arabic.

2

Understand Chapters 5, 6 and 8

Raining cats and dogs is an idiom: it has nothing to do with animals and simply means “raining heavily”. Match here means a game: مباراة.

3

Translate Chapters 7–13

Word by word ✗ إنها تمطر قططاً وكلاباً، لذلك تمّ إلغاء المباراة.

By meaning ✓ أُلغيت المباراة بسبب الأمطار الغزيرة.

4

Check Chapters 15 and 16

Read the Arabic without the English. Is the meaning complete? ✓ Does it sound like natural Arabic? ✓ It starts with a verb and avoids the heavy تمّ.

5

Explain Chapter 17

“The idiom was translated by its meaning, because a word-for-word version would make no sense to Arab readers. The sentence was also rearranged so that it starts with a verb, as Arabic prefers.”

Remember
  • Prepare → Understand → Translate → Check → Explain. Every chapter in this book helps with one of these steps.
