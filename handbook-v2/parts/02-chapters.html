Chapter 1

What is translation?

ما الترجمة؟

This chapter explains what translation is and why meaning matters more than words.

Key terms

Translationالترجمة التحريريةTransferring the meaning of a written text into another language.
Interpretationالترجمة الشفهيةTransferring spoken language into another language.
CommunicationالتواصلUnderstanding and being understood.

1.1Translation carries meaning

A translator does not replace words one by one. A translator carries the meaning of a text from one language to another, so that a reader in the other language understands it.

So translation is a kind of communication between two languages. If the reader does not understand the translation, it has failed.

1.2Translation is approximation

No two languages match perfectly. The translator’s job is to bring the meaning as close as possible to the target language, especially its cultural meaning (Chapter 12).

1.3The translator in the middle

The author writes for readers of one language. The translator reads the text, understands it, and rewrites it for readers of another language.

Remember
  • Translate the meaning, not the words.
  • Always think about the reader.

Chapter 2

The translator

المترجم

This chapter describes the skills a translator needs and the two ways a translator can work.

Key terms

Commissioned translatorمترجم بتكليفTranslates for a client.
Self-initiated translatorمترجم بمبادرة ذاتيةChooses a text to translate, with no client.
Biculturalمُلِمّ بالثقافتينKnows both cultures well.

2.1What a good translator needs

  • Two languages. Be fluent in both the source and the target language.
  • Two cultures. Cultural knowledge is as important as language knowledge.
  • General knowledge. A translator should be like a “walking encyclopedia”.
  • A rich vocabulary (الحصيلة اللغوية), including idioms and fixed expressions.
  • Practice. Skill grows with experience and with revising your work.

2.2Two ways of working

Commissioned translatorSelf-initiated translator
A client asks you to translate. You follow the client’s style and policy.Nobody asks you. You choose the text yourself, for example a poem you love.

A freelancer works for clients, so a freelancer is a commissioned translator.

2.3Five good habits

  1. Be faithful to the meaning (الأمانة).
  2. Aim to be accurate, faithful and acceptable to the reader.
  3. Remember that translation is rewriting: the text must read naturally.
  4. Revise every translation.
  5. Translate everything, including titles and captions.
Remember
  • A translator needs two languages and two cultures.
  • Revise every translation, even your best one.

Chapter 3

Before you translate: the brief

قبل أن تترجم: موجز الترجمة

This chapter shows you what to find out, and what to do, before you start translating.

Key terms

Translation briefموجز الترجمةThe information you need before you start: client, purpose and audience.
Target audienceالجمهور المستهدفThe people who will read the translation.

3.1Ask three questions

QuestionWhy it matters
Who is the client? من هو العميل؟Each client has its own style and policy.
What is the purpose? ما الغرض؟Is the text meant to inform, persuade, instruct or entertain?
Who is the audience? من هو الجمهور؟Experts or the general public? Adults or children?

3.2Prepare in five steps

  1. Read the whole text twice before you translate.
  2. Highlight difficult words, idioms and names.
  3. Research the topic.
  4. Make a list of the key terms (Chapter 14).
  5. Picture a first draft in your mind.

3.3An example brief

TextA short news report
ClientKUNA (Kuwait News Agency)
PurposeTo inform
AudienceEnglish readers interested in Kuwait
So…Use a formal, neutral style and keep the headline short.
Remember
  • No brief, no plan: find out the client, purpose and audience first.
  • Never start translating before you have read the whole text.

Chapter 4

English and Arabic are different

الفروق بين الإنجليزية والعربية

This chapter compares the two languages, so that you can avoid the most common problems.

Key terms

Verbal sentenceالجملة الفعليةA sentence that starts with a verb.
Registerالمستوى اللغويHow formal or informal the language is.
Modern Standard Arabicالفصحى المعاصرةThe Arabic of newspapers, laws and education.

4.1The main differences

EnglishArabic
Word orderSubject first: Sam ate the cake.Verb first: أكلَ سامٌ الكعكةَ
SentencesShort, with full stopsLong, joined with و، ف، ثمّ
The verb “is”Always writtenOften not written, or يُعدّ
SynonymsOne word is enoughPairs add emphasis: شجاعٌ مِغوار
Punctuation, ; ?، ؛ ؟

4.2Which Arabic?

Use Modern Standard Arabic for written translation. Use a dialect (العامية) only if the client asks for it, for example in film subtitles.

4.3Small words, big traps EN → AR

EnglishArabicWhy
He is in bed.إنه نائمIt means “asleep”, not في السرير.
As an officer, I…بصفتي ضابطاً…Here as means a role, not كـ.
Translation is a fascinating field.تُعدّ الترجمة مجالاً شائقاًStart with a verb.
…to see the Roman ruinsلزيارة الآثار الرومانيةArabic says “visit”.

4.4Linking words EN → AR

Arabic joins ideas more than English does. These linking words help your Arabic flow:

EnglishArabicEnglishArabic
howeverغير أنّthereforeلذلك
in additionبالإضافة إلى ذلكfor exampleعلى سبيل المثال
on the other handمن ناحيةٍ أخرىas a resultونتيجةً لذلك

4.5Numbers

With numbers from 3 to 10, the number takes the opposite gender to the noun: ثلاثةُ طلابٍ but ثلاثُ طالباتٍ.

Remember
  • EN → AR: start with the verb and join short sentences.
  • AR → EN: start with the subject, drop repeated synonyms and split long sentences.
  • Use Arabic punctuation in Arabic texts: ، ؛ ؟

Chapter 5

Meaning, form and context

المعنى والبناء والسياق

This chapter explains the most important rule in translation: meaning comes before form.

Key terms

FormالبناءThe structure of the words: word, phrase, sentence.
MeaningالمعنىThe idea that the words express.
Denotationالمعنى المعجميThe basic dictionary meaning.
Connotationالمعنى الإيحائيThe feeling a word carries: positive, negative, respectful.
ContextالسياقThe words and situation around a text.

5.1Meaning comes before form

You may change the structure of a sentence, but you must never change its meaning (المعنى أهمّ من البناء).

EnglishArabicWhy
Blair, Bush’s poodleبلير، تابعُ بوش“Poodle” has no political meaning in Arabic, so we keep the idea: “an obedient follower”.
He is a heavy smoker.يدخّن بشراهةThe adjective becomes a verb, and the meaning stays the same.

5.2Same meaning, different feeling

WordFeeling
die ماتNeutral
pass away توفّيRespectful
kill قتل · assassinate اغتال“Assassinate” is political
friend صديق · comrade رفيق“Comrade” means a member of the same party

5.3What the speaker really means

Sometimes a speaker means more than the words say:

WordsReal meaning
أشعرُ بالحرّ“Please turn on the air conditioning.”
It’s freezing in here.“Please close the window.”

5.4Context tells you which meaning

Many words have more than one meaning. The context tells you which one is correct (لكلّ مقامٍ مقال).

WordMeanings
bankمصرف (a financial bank) · ضفّة النهر (a river bank)
عينan eye · a water spring · a spy
caseقضية (in court) · حالة (in medicine) · علبة (a box)
Remember
  • Change the form if you need to, but never change the meaning.
  • Check the connotation, not only the dictionary meaning.
  • Let the context decide which meaning is correct.

Chapter 6

Analysing the text

تحليل النص

This chapter shows you how to study a text carefully before you translate it.

Key terms

Text analysisتحليل النصStudying a text before translating it.
Genreالجنس النصّيThe type of text: news, contract, poem, advert…
ToneالنبرةThe writer’s attitude: neutral, critical, friendly…

6.1Why analyse first?

  • You understand the text better with every reading.
  • You can find the difficult parts in advance.
  • You can choose your strategies before you start.
  • You can decide which resources you need.

6.2Analysis is not a summary

AnalysisSummary
Describes the text: who wrote it, for whom, why, and how.Only says what the text is about.

When you are asked to analyse, do not summarise.

6.3Ten analysis questions

Answer these questions for every new text. The example answers are for a short KUNA news report.

QuestionExample answer
1. Who wrote it, and who published it?A KUNA reporter; KUNA
2. Who is the client?KUNA’s English service
3. What is the purpose?To inform
4. Who is the audience?Readers interested in Kuwait
5. What is the genre?A news report
6. What is the field?Politics
7. What are the tone and style?Neutral and formal
8. Which terms need checking?Official titles
9. Are there any cultural items?Forms of address
10. Which strategies and resources will I use?Mostly literal; a news-style guide

A blank copy of these questions is on the worksheet page at the back of the book.

Remember
  • Analyse before you translate.
  • Your translation must match your analysis: if you call the tone formal, keep it formal.

Chapter 10

The seven translation procedures

إجراءات الترجمة السبعة

This chapter presents seven techniques for translating words and phrases, from the closest to the source to the freest.

These seven procedures were described by Vinay and Darbelnet. Procedures 1–3 stay close to the source. Procedures 4–7 move further away, and you use them when a close translation sounds wrong.

ProcedureWhat it doesExample
1Borrowing
الاقتراض		Take the word as it is.computer → كمبيوتر
2Calque
النسخ		Translate each part of the expression.skyscraper → ناطحة سحاب
3Literal translation
الترجمة الحرفية		Translate word for word, when this is correct.The book is on the table. → الكتاب على الطاولة
4Transposition
الإبدال		Change the word class, e.g. noun → verb.after his arrival → بعد أن وصل
5Modulation
التحوير		Change the point of view.It is not difficult. → إنه سهل
6Equivalence
التكافؤ		Use a completely different fixed expression.Like father, like son. → هذا الشبل من ذاك الأسد
7Adaptation
التكييف		Replace a cultural situation with a familiar one.like Romeo and Juliet → كقيسٍ وليلى

1–3 = direct procedures · 4–7 = oblique procedures

10.1The procedures in one sentence

After his arrival in Kuwait, the minister held a press conference at the Sheraton and said the reforms were not impossible.

بعد أن وصل الوزير إلى الكويت، عقد مؤتمراً صحفياً في فندق شيراتون، وقال إنّ الإصلاحات ممكنة.

EnglishArabicProcedure
after his arrivalبعد أن وصلTransposition (noun → verb)
press conferenceمؤتمر صحفيCalque
SheratonشيراتونBorrowing
not impossibleممكنةModulation

10.2Why learn the names?

When you write a commentary (Chapter 17), you can name the procedure you used and explain why. For example: “After his arrival” was changed into a verb because Arabic prefers verbs.

Remember
  • Start with procedures 1–3.
  • Move to 4–7 when the result sounds unnatural or unclear.

Chapter 11

Translation strategies

استراتيجيات الترجمة

This chapter gives you practical solutions for words and expressions that have no direct equivalent.

Key terms

StrategyالاستراتيجيةA planned solution to a translation problem.

11.1Six useful strategies

StrategyUse it when…Example
Functional translation
الترجمة الوظيفية		a TL expression does the same jobspill the beans → أفشى السرّ
Cultural substitution
الإبدال الثقافي		a cultural reference would mean nothing to the readerRomeo and Juliet → قيس وليلى
Addition
الإضافة		the reader needs extra information (common EN → AR)the Fed → مجلس الاحتياطي الفيدرالي الأمريكي
Omission
الحذف		words repeat the same meaning (common AR → EN)شجاعٌ مِغوار → very brave
A more general word
اللفظ الأعمّ		the TL has no exact wordprawns / shrimps → روبيان
Borrowing + explanation
الاقتراض مع الشرح		a cultural word must be keptالوقف → waqf (a charitable endowment)

Never add information to headlines, and never omit real information.

11.2Translating metaphors

A metaphor (الاستعارة) compares two things without “as” or “like”: She is a moon. A simile (التشبيه) uses “as” or “like”. Follow these four steps, in order:

StepExample
1. Keep the image if both languages share it.She is a snake. → إنها أفعى
2. Use an Arabic image if the English one is unfamiliar.the tip of the iceberg → غيضٌ من فيض
3. Change it into a simile.He is a rock. → إنه كالصخرة في ثباته
4. Give the plain meaning (the last option).He is a chicken. → إنه جبان

11.3Domestication and foreignisation

Domestication (التوطين) makes the text feel local to the reader. Foreignisation (التغريب) keeps its foreign flavour. For example, the diwaniya (foreignised) or a men’s evening gathering (domesticated).

Remember
  • Choose a strategy for each problem, not for the whole text.
  • For metaphors: keep the image, replace it, turn it into a simile, or explain it, in that order.

Spotting risks

This chapter helps you find the places where a translation mistake could cause real harm.

Key terms

RiskالخطرThe chance that a translation choice causes a problem.

Before you deliver a translation, look for these risky places:

RiskExampleWhat to do
Wrong termgoverning law → القانون الحاكمCheck a specialised dictionary
Lost “not”A negative sentence becomes positiveCompare sentence by sentence
Cultural imagewarmed my heart → أدفأ قلبيUse cultural approximation

5. JustificationWhy is your choice right?

17.2An example

The ST says the agreement “remained a dead letter”. A literal translation, حرفاً ميتاً, would confuse Arab readers, so the translator chose حبراً على ورق. This is a functional equivalent. It was checked in Arabic newspapers. It is a familiar idiom and suits the formal style of the news report.

17.3Rules

  • Write in the third person: “The translator…”
  • Quote examples and name the strategy.
  • Always explain why.
Remember
  • Problem → solution → strategy → resource → justification.

This chapter shows you which dictionaries to use and how to use them well.

Key terms

19.1Useful dictionaries

DictionaryUse it for
Al-Mawrid (English–Arabic, Arabic–English)Everyday words in both directions

Online dictionaries (e.g. Almaany)Quick searches. Always double-check them.

19.2The three-check rule

  1. Find options in a bilingual dictionary.
  2. Confirm the meaning in a monolingual dictionary.
  3. Check real use in a text written in the TL.

Example: for fast colour, a bilingual dictionary gives سريع، ثابت. An Arabic dictionary confirms that ثابت means “does not fade”. So the answer is لون ثابت.

Remember
  • Never take the first meaning a dictionary gives.
  • Check in a bilingual dictionary, then a monolingual one, then a real text.
