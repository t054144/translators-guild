Text analysis

تحليل النص قبل الترجمة
Team notes

To analyse a text is to break it down and understand it before you translate. A text can be anything written: a word, a sentence, a paragraph or a book. Use this page with any unfamiliar text. The grey italic notes show a completed example for a KUNA news report.

PublisherالناشرWhere was it published?KUNA, the state news agency
ClientالعميلWho asked for the translation?KUNA’s English service
PurposeالغرضInform, persuade, instruct?To inform
Target audienceالجمهورWho will read the TT?Readers interested in Kuwait
GenreالجنسWhat kind of text is it?A news report
FieldالمجالWhat subject area?Politics
ToneالنبرةNeutral, critical, warm?Straightforward, neutral
StyleالأسلوبFormal or informal?Formal, with no short forms
ContextالسياقWhen, where, why?An official visit
Key termsالمصطلحاتWhich words need checking?Ministers’ official titles
Cultural elementsالعناصر الثقافيةAnything culture-bound?Forms of address
Potential problemsالصعوباتWhat might go wrong?Connotation, place names
StrategiesالاستراتيجياتHow will you solve them?Mostly literal (no metaphors)
Resources neededالمصادرWhich dictionaries and tools?UNTERM, the KUNA archive
Analysis
Shows the text’s features, background and problems: who, for whom, how and why.
Not a summary
A summary only says what the text is about. Do not summarise when you are asked to analyse.
Translator’s note
Your analysis and your translation must agree. If you call the tone formal, a single casual word in your TT will stand out.

Part IV · Text and Discourse · Step 3: Comment

Translation commentary

التعليق على الترجمة
Team notes
Concept
Commentary التعليق
A short text in which the translator explains and justifies the choices made in a translation.

Every translation goes through three steps: Analysis → Translation → Commentary. For each problem in the commentary, follow these five moves:

A model commentary Example

[Problem] The ST says the agreement “remained a dead letter”. [Solution] A literal rendering, حرفاً ميتاً, would confuse Arab readers, so the translator chose حبراً على ورق. [Strategy] This is a functional equivalent. [Resource] Its use was checked in Arabic press texts. [Justification] It is a familiar Arabic idiom and suits the formal news register.

Structure

Part one: reflect on your analysis (text type, audience, main problems).

Part two: reflect on your translation, one problem at a time.

Rules

  • Write in the third person: “The translator…”
  • Quote examples from the ST and TT.
  • Name the strategy or procedure.
  • Mention the dictionaries you used.

Useful phrases

The ST features…
To keep…, the translator opted for…
A functional equivalent was preferred because…
This was omitted as it is irrelevant to the target audience.
The term was checked in…
Common error
Describing the translation (“I translated the first line…”) without saying why. Every comment needs a reason.
Translator’s note
Commenting on someone else’s translation? Compare the ST and TT line by line, note what was added, dropped or changed, then judge the result for accuracy, terms, fluency and fitness for the reader. Separate real errors from personal taste.

Interlingual risk assessment

تقييم المخاطر بين اللغتين
Requested in the team notesAdded framework: risk management (Pym 2015)
Concept
Risk الخطر
The chance that a translation choice causes a problem for the client, the reader or you.
Mitigation التخفيف
An action that makes the risk smaller.

Before you deliver, go through this matrix. Impact asks how bad the problem would be. Likelihood asks when it tends to happen.

RiskExampleImpactLikelihoodMitigation

Plot your own risks

Place each risky item from your translation in the grid. Accept low risks, check medium ones against a source, get a second person to review high ones, and flag critical ones to the client.

The grid is a thinking tool, not a measurement. How high a risk is depends on the text and the client.

Fairclough’s relational model

نموذج فيركلف العلائقي في التحليل النقدي للخطاب
Requested in the team notesAdded framework: Fairclough (1992, 2009)
Concept
Discourse الخطاب
Language in use, in real social situations.
Critical discourse analysis التحليل النقدي للخطاب
Studying how language shows, supports or challenges power and ideology.
Ideology الأيديولوجيا
A set of beliefs that shapes how events are described.

Fairclough looks at every text on three levels, one inside the other. He calls the approach relational because the text, its production and society all shape one another.

Why translators care: a translation is also discourse. Your word choices can keep, soften or strengthen the stance of the ST.

Loaded choices EN → AR

regime · governmentنظام · حكومة“Regime” takes away legitimacy
militants · terrorists · fightersمسلّحون · إرهابيون · مقاتلونEach one takes a side
Protesters were killed.قُتل متظاهرونThe passive hides who did it

☐ Fairclough checklist

  • Text: which words carry judgement?
  • Text: who is the doer, and is the doer hidden?
  • Discourse: who produced it, and for which outlet?
  • Society: whose interests does it serve?
  • Your TT: did you keep, soften or strengthen the stance?
  • Your TT: did you flag loaded terms?
Key takeaway
No word choice is neutral. When the ST takes a stance, notice it, follow the client’s policy, and explain your choice in the commentary.
