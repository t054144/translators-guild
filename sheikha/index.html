The destination

A booth at the United Nations.

A conference interpreter. Political, international. The room where the sentence has to be right the first time, because there is no second time.

I follow international politics closely. Interpretation is where the two things I care about meet — language under pressure, and what happens when it fails.

No certificate for it yet. No course to take here. For now: watching, listening, and waiting for the door.

So everything after it arrives second. I'm aware that's not a fair standard. I'm keeping it anyway.

The other language

Code & cybersecurity

A language is a system of rules that produces meaning, and so is a program. The difference is the penalty for a mistake: in Arabic, an error is a bad sentence. In code, it's a door someone walks through.

That is what pulled me toward security. Translation taught me to read something and ask what it's really doing — that is the same instinct.

Coded

Courses in programming, cybersecurity, and AI-assisted development. Four certificates.
