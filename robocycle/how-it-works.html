Why electronic waste needs separate handling

A lithium battery in a household bin does not stay intact. It is tipped, compacted and crushed on its way through collection and sorting, and a damaged cell can short internally and enter thermal runaway — which is how fires start in bin lorries and waste facilities.

Incorrect disposal also releases hazardous material into air, soil and water, and throws away metals and components that are still good.

Keeping electronics out of general waste removes the hazard and keeps the useful parts in circulation. That is the whole idea.
