The problem

Electronic waste accumulates where people live.

Stored electronics are not inert. Lithium cells swell and vent as they age, damaged cables arc under load, and overloaded power strips carry devices that were never meant to stay connected. Alongside the fire risk sits the slower one: heavy metals in household waste, and recoverable material thrown away.

Why this starts with a fire

A drawer of old electronics is a store of energy.

The opening film is not a dramatisation of something rare. Fires that begin in stored consumer electronics are a recognised hazard, and they share the same few causes — which is exactly why they are avoidable.

Ageing lithium cells

A lithium battery does not become safe by being unused. Cells swell, vent and can enter thermal runaway — and a swollen battery in a drawer sits beside everything else in it.

Damaged cables

Frayed or crushed cables arc under load. In a tangle of chargers, the damaged one is indistinguishable from the rest until it fails.

Overloaded strips

Power strips carrying old devices and daisy-chained adapters run hot, often behind furniture where nobody sees the heat build.

Unventilated storage

Cupboards, boxes and under-bed storage keep the heat in and the warning signs — smell, discolouration, buzzing — out of sight.

Until you can bring them in

  • Stop charging anything unattended overnight, especially older devices.
  • Take a swollen, hot or leaking battery out of the house — do not bin it, and do not puncture it.
  • Don't daisy-chain power strips, and keep them clear of fabric and furniture.
  • Replace any cable with damaged insulation rather than taping it.
  • Keep loose batteries apart, with their terminals taped, so they cannot short against each other.

General safety guidance, not a substitute for your local fire authority. In an emergency in Kuwait, call 112.

The solution

A collection system, not a campaign.

Robocycle runs the full path a device takes after it leaves the home — and keeps that path visible to the person who handed it over.

01

Drop it at a booth

Community collection booths sited where people already go: malls, co-ops, university campuses and municipal buildings. No appointment, no fee.

02

Earn on deposit

Each accepted device earns points, weighted by what can be recovered from it. Points convert to partner credit — responsible disposal pays.

03

Track what happens next

Every unit gets an ID at the booth. Follow it through intake, triage, component recovery and reuse, from the device page.

04

Components go back out

Working parts are identified, catalogued and redirected to student, robotics and research projects instead of being shredded for scrap.

Recovery

A dead laptop is a parts supply.

Triage separates what still works from what does not. Screens, drives, memory, motors, sensors, connectors and controllers are tested, graded and listed — then routed to projects that would otherwise buy the same parts new.

Identified

Each intake is opened and inventoried. Components are tested against their spec, not guessed at from the model number.

Catalogued

Graded parts enter a searchable inventory with their condition, origin device and the tests they passed.

Redirected

Schools, university labs and robotics teams in Kuwait request parts from the catalogue. Material that cannot be reused goes to licensed processing.

Give them away

The device doing nothing in your drawer is worth something to someone else.

You are not throwing it away. A working screen becomes a student's monitor, a sound motor becomes part of a robotics project, and a dead battery stops being a hazard in your home. Bringing it in takes minutes and costs nothing.

What we take

Phones, laptops, tablets, chargers, cables, power strips, routers, consoles, cameras, small appliances and loose batteries — working or not, whole or not.

Your data comes first

Sign out and factory-reset if the device still powers on. If it doesn't, say so at the booth: storage is removed and wiped before anything is triaged, and drives that cannot be wiped are destroyed.

What you get back

Points at deposit, weighted by what can be recovered, and a tracking ID so you can see where your device went rather than taking our word for it.
